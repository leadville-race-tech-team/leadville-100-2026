2026 aid stations — leg by leg

miles & cutoffs from the official chart · distance, gain and loss measured from the route GPX

The two GPX downloads. Both carry identical geometry (the official RideWithGPS route 56633050, 4,858 points, unrounded) and the same 89 cue and 7 aid-station waypoints. They differ only in the elevation stream. Official course GPX is the default: RideWithGPS DEM elevation, passed through byte-for-byte, reproducing the published 13,552 ft gain figure. Measured-elevation GPX replaces that with barometric readings from a 2025 race recording across the 68.5 mi the two courses share (ground new in 2026 keeps DEM). Barometric readings were measured on site rather than modelled. After removing the recording’s own drift and locking each lookup to a single 2025 pass, they imply 13,405 ft of gain — 144 ft less than the terrain model. Use the default unless you specifically want measured point elevations.

Sources: 2025 — David Roche’s Strava race recording (“Leadville 100 Mile CR, 15:12:30”); 2026 — official RideWithGPS route “2026 LT 100 RUN”, as listed on the Leadville 100 site. Surface types (paved / unpaved / unknown) are from that RideWithGPS route’s per-point surface data, which derives from OpenStreetMap.

* How the elevation numbers are made. Gain is counted by walking the elevation stream and adding every rise that exceeds ±7.5 m, which filters sensor and sampling noise. The ±7.5 m figure is not arbitrary: it is the value at which the official 2026 route reproduces the organiser’s published 13,552 ft, so it is calibrated to their method. The same threshold is used everywhere here. Official reads elevation from the 2026 route’s terrain model — a value looked up from a digital elevation model at each point, never measured on site. Measured replaces that with barometric readings recorded by a device carried through the 2025 race, applied across the ground the two courses share and left as terrain-model data on ground new in 2026. The 2025 column is barometric in both views, because a barometer is the only record of that run — which is why the Official pairing compares two different kinds of measurement and the Measured pairing does not. The gap between the two matters, so two things are done before any lookup. The recording’s barometric drift is removed using the fact that it starts and finishes at the same intersection, and each lookup is locked to one 2025 pass at a time. Both are necessary because much of the course runs out and back: the two 2025 passes sit within metres of each other but were recorded hours apart, and mixing them injects that drift as climb that never happened. With that done the barometer reads 144 ft less gain than the terrain model, and stays below it at every filtering threshold. Treat the individual totals as method-dependent, and the Measured pairing as the one to compare years with. Shared sections are drawn once, following the 2026 line. “Changed” = farther than ~70 m from every point of the other year’s track, for at least a quarter mile.

Aid-station miles, cutoffs and crew access are from the 2026 Run Course tab on leadvilleraceseries.com (fetched Aug 14, 2026): 12 stations, and crewing is allowed only at Outward Bound, Pipeline and Twin Lakes Village (each visited twice; Twin Lakes is shuttle-access only from Outward Bound). Cutoff times without a day are Saturday; the course cutoff is 30 hours. The site’s drop-bag tab has not been updated for the 2026 reroute — it still lists the pre-fire locations (Mayqueen, Outward Bound, Half Pipe, Twin Lakes, Winfield). Drop bags are shown here where the names match 2026 stations; Pipeline is marked “?” because the old list names Half Pipe, which is not a 2026 station.
